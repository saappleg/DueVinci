What to expect from integrations

  • Canvas: choose which courses to sync; their assignments are imported directly. Optional assignment-change alerts can follow a manual or scheduled refresh; posted-grade alerts compare changes after the first observation. Disconnecting stops future refreshes and alerts. Imported work remains in your planner.
  • Brightspace: the current connection uses a private calendar feed for SNHU events and dates; it does not create course records or Smart Plan tasks.
  • Calendar files and accounts: importing an .ics file copies its events into DueVinci. Google Calendar and Outlook sync the default calendar once after you connect, then when you start a later sync. They import event titles and start dates in a bounded date window and do not write planner changes back. Disconnecting hides those imported events from DueVinci.

Privacy and control

AI syllabus import is optional and sends the selected syllabus text or image to Google Gemini through a server-side service. Connected calendar titles and dates, encrypted provider tokens, and minimal Canvas grade snapshots are processed to provide optional sync and alerts. Review extracted details before saving them, and do not submit highly sensitive personal information. Backups are plain JSON files, so store them somewhere you trust. For data details, retention, and disconnect behavior, read the Privacy Policy.

Nursing tools are for fictional study practice only. Do not enter real patient details or use them for patient care.

Common questions

Can I use DueVinci without paying?

Yes. Course setup, deadlines, planning, grades, calendar tools, timers, and portable backups are part of the free core. Pro is optional.

Does everything work offline?

The cached app and supported planner edits can work offline after you have loaded DueVinci online. Authentication, AI, billing, and connected-provider actions need a connection. Wait for the sync status to clear before relying on another device.

Does the GPA match my school’s transcript?

The calculator supports weighted grade categories and course credits, then converts course percentages linearly to a selected 4.0 or 5.0 scale. Schools use different grading and repeat-course policies, so check your institution’s official calculation for academic decisions.
